## Consulting should produce an investment decision

An SME needs to know where AI helps now and what is not worth building. A consultant begins with the objective: reduce response time, improve invoice handling, search company policy, or support sales. They measure the present condition and identify the systems, data, and people who own the outcome.

## What happens in a readiness assessment?

The consultant interviews process owners, maps steps and exceptions, and inventories data sources and existing tools. The first question is not which model the company prefers. It is where time is lost and how improvement will be recognized. Assessment also examines quality, access, sensitivity, and dependency on external providers.

A useful output is not a mysterious readiness score. It states what is ready, what needs cleanup or policy, and what should be excluded because risk is high or impact is absent. It names owners of decisions, review, and support. Discovering that straightforward automation or a CRM correction solves the problem without AI is evidence of independent judgment, not missing value.

## Rank use cases visibly

Collect ideas from business teams, then compare impact, implementation ease, and risk. Impact includes measurable time, cost, quality, or revenue. Ease includes available data, connection options, and an accountable owner. Risk covers privacy, error consequences, material decisions, and reputation. The reasons behind ranking should remain visible rather than disappearing into one score.

A good first case might summarize support requests with employee approval, retrieve an answer from approved internal policy, or classify documents. An automated hiring or financial decision based on weak records is a poor starting point. ## Use a prototype to test feasibility

A prototype tests one hypothesis with the smallest useful scope. It uses an authorized representative sample, a basic interface, and a before-and-after measure. Testing should include normal cases, failures, and unauthorized-access attempts. Never confuse a demonstration using carefully selected examples with evidence from a sample resembling everyday work.

Define acceptance, such as classification quality checked by a specialist or faster search without disclosing a restricted source. Record an estimated operating cost, volume assumptions, and ownership of failed outputs. A prototype is not production; it may still need identity, authorization, monitoring, backup, training, and support. Consulting must distinguish what was proved from what remains unknown.

## Independent consultant or implementation agency?

An experienced independent consultant may fit an impartial review, a specialist question, or short training, usually with direct communication. Their ability to cover experience design, data, development, security, and support may depend on additional partners. An implementation agency brings disciplines together and can turn advice into an operated system, commonly with broader scope.

Do not assume an agency is automatically better. Evaluate the people who will perform the work rather than the logo. Ask who creates the prototype, reviews information, and supports production. If the supplier both advises and builds, require a decision gate after assessment so implementation is not predetermined. Compare code and account ownership and the ability to move to another supplier.

## Warning signs of weak advice

Be cautious with promised returns without a baseline, tools proposed before process discovery, or one presentation sold to every sector. Other signs include avoiding data and security, refusing to define deliverables, demonstrating only selected examples, and treating conversation volume as success. Do not accept unsupported client numbers, awards, or performance percentages.

Weak consulting leaves a company with product names but no next step, owner, or operating cost. The consultant may depend on a personal account or retain code and credentials. Require company-owned repositories and accounts, decision and test records, and an exit plan. Good advice reduces uncertainty. If terminology grows while the decision remains obscure, the output is inadequate.

## When an SME does not need a consultant

A full engagement is unnecessary when the objective is employee training on a general ready-made feature, experimentation without internal data, or simple documented automation that IT can deliver. The company can set policy and measurement internally. Advice also fails when management provides no owner, decision time, or data access.

The gap may call for a trainer, integration engineer, or security specialist rather than a general adviser. Define whether you need a decision, skill, build, or risk review. If the underlying process is chaotic, improve it first or include process correction in scope. External consulting never replaces internal leadership accountable for adoption and results.
