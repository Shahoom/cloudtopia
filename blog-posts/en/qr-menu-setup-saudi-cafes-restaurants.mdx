Every café in Riyadh, Jeddah, and Dammam has a QR menu taped to the table. Most of them are PDFs uploaded to random hosting, loading 6 MB images over 4G, with broken Arabic typography. The customer leaves the table, not the menu.

A proper QR menu is a full digital ordering experience: bilingual, image-optimized, fast, with optional order-and-pay. Here's how to build one that actually converts.

<Callout tone="info" title="Short answer">
A good Saudi QR menu in 2026 costs **SAR 800–2,500 for a browse-only menu**, **SAR 3,000–6,000 with order-to-kitchen**, and **SAR 5,000–15,000 with full order-and-pay + ZATCA-compliant receipts**. Avoid PDF-on-Google-Drive setups — they kill conversion.
</Callout>

## What a proper QR menu needs

1. **Bilingual AR+EN by default.** Arabic speakers get Arabic first; tourists and expats get English. Same menu, toggled.
2. **Optimized photos.** WebP or AVIF, lazy-loaded, CDN-served. Each item image under 80 KB. A 6 MB PDF menu is 75× larger than it needs to be.
3. **Fast first paint.** Under 1.5 seconds on 4G. Customers scan and want food fast.
4. **Category navigation.** Sticky category tabs, clear pricing, allergen info where relevant.
5. **Mada + Apple Pay** if you offer pay-at-table. See our [Gulf payment gateways guide](/en/blog/gulf-payment-gateways-mada-stc-pay-tabby).
6. **ZATCA-compliant e-receipt** if you're VAT-registered. See our [ZATCA Phase 2 checklist](/en/blog/zatca-phase-2-e-invoicing-developer-checklist).
7. **Kitchen printer integration** if ordering to kitchen. Star or Epson thermal printers over LAN/Bluetooth.
8. **Admin dashboard** to update menu items without calling the developer every time.

## Common mistakes we see

- **PDF menus:** 5–10 MB files, no search, broken Arabic on iOS PDF viewer.
- **Foreign platforms without Arabic:** Menu pictures with the English-first layout, Arabic translation bolted on.
- **No photos:** 40%+ conversion drop versus menus with good food photography.
- **No price on menu card:** Customers resent having to open a separate page to see price.
- **Heavy hero videos:** Auto-playing 10 MB hero videos on a QR menu is hostile.

<Callout tone="tip" title="Photograph your menu in batches">
Budget one half-day with a food photographer (SAR 2,000–4,000) to shoot every item on a neutral background plus 3–5 lifestyle shots. Reuse across QR menu, Instagram, Google Business Profile, and any delivery app.
</Callout>

<FAQ title="Common questions about Saudi QR menus">
  <Q q="How much should a café spend on a QR menu?">
    SAR 800–2,500 for browse-only, SAR 3,000–6,000 with ordering. Anything above SAR 10,000 usually includes multi-branch + POS integration.
  </Q>
  <Q q="Do I need Mada and Apple Pay for a QR menu?">
    Only if you enable pay-at-table. For browse-and-call-waiter, no. For self-service order + pay, yes.
  </Q>
  <Q q="Can I use Foodics or Marn?">
    Yes — good POS-native options, especially for chains. Monthly subscription is the trade-off. For a single-location independent café, custom is often cheaper over 2 years.
  </Q>
  <Q q="Is PDF really that bad?">
    Yes. Slow, no search, broken Arabic on some iOS PDF viewers, no analytics, no ordering, no updates without reprinting. Don't.
  </Q>
  <Q q="What if I just want to update prices easily?">
    Any admin-backed menu (custom or SaaS) lets you update prices in 30 seconds. PDF means you reprint QR stickers every time you change a price.
  </Q>
  <Q q="Can the QR menu print to my kitchen printer?">
    Yes — via LAN or Bluetooth connection to a Star/Epson thermal printer, or via POS integration. Budget 1–2 days of setup per location.
  </Q>
  <Q q="Does Arabic SEO matter for a QR menu?">
    For walk-in tables, no. But your QR menu URL is often shared on Google Business Profile, Instagram, and WhatsApp — Arabic SEO on that URL helps local discovery.
  </Q>
</FAQ>
