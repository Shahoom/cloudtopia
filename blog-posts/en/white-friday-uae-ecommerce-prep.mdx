White Friday (the Gulf version of Black Friday) typically runs the last week of November. For UAE ecommerce, it's the single largest revenue day of the year. Stores that don't prepare lose money in two ways: lost sales when the site crashes, and wasted ad spend on broken funnels.

Here's an 8-week prep plan we run with UAE ecommerce clients.

<Callout tone="info" title="The short plan">
8 weeks before: infrastructure stress test. 6 weeks: creative and paid media warmup. 4 weeks: landing pages, email lists, inventory planning. 2 weeks: final creative, VIP early access. White Friday week: execute, monitor, respond. Post: retention, returns, analysis.
</Callout>

## Week 8–7: infrastructure

- Load test the site at 5× normal peak traffic.
- CDN + caching reviewed. Cold cache clear and re-warm plan.
- Checkout error monitoring. Budget alerts on payment PSP failures.
- Mobile performance baseline — Lighthouse 85+ on product and checkout.

## Week 6: creative production

- Hero campaign shoot. Bilingual AR+EN assets.
- Product photography refresh for hero SKUs.
- Ad creative variants: 6 per platform (Meta, Google, Snap, TikTok).

## Week 5: inventory and logistics

- SKU-level inventory forecast for peak week.
- Shipping SLA contracts with Aramex/Fetchr confirmed for spike volume.
- Cash-on-delivery limits set with logistics to avoid refusals.

## Week 4: landing pages

- `/white-friday` and `/early-access` live with collection structure.
- Indexed via sitemap. Not heavily promoted yet.
- Countdown timer component built and tested.

## Week 3: email + VIP warm-up

- VIP early-access email: 48 hours of deals before public launch.
- Paid retargeting segments created for VIP, cart abandoner, past buyer.
- Push notification stack tested (web push + WhatsApp).

## Week 2: paid media warmup

- Brand-lift campaigns to cold audiences.
- Lookalike audiences built from buyers.
- Ad creative approved and queued.

## Week 1: final prep

- Final creative live.
- Customer service scripts in AR+EN for expected queries.
- Returns policy clearly communicated on site.
- On-call rotation set for White Friday day.

## White Friday week: execute

- Traffic monitoring in real time.
- Ad budget reallocation every 2 hours based on ROAS.
- Inventory re-checks every 4 hours.
- Customer service response SLA under 30 minutes.

## Post-White Friday (2 weeks)

- Returns and refund flow at peak — staff accordingly.
- Retention email to new buyers.
- Analysis: what converted, what didn't, learnings for next year.

<Callout tone="tip" title="The biggest UAE White Friday mistake">
Not stress-testing the checkout on Mada/Apple Pay/Tabby flows. Most sites crash at the payment step, not the browse step. Run 20 real test orders in the week before — across card, Apple Pay, and BNPL.
</Callout>

<FAQ title="Common questions about UAE White Friday prep">
  <Q q="When is White Friday UAE 2026?">
    Last Friday of November 2026 (November 27). Most retailers run sales for a 5–10 day window around it.
  </Q>
  <Q q="How much traffic spike should I expect?">
    Category-dependent. Electronics 5–10×, fashion 3–5×, beauty 3–4×, home 2–3× versus a normal Friday.
  </Q>
  <Q q="What's the biggest technical risk?">
    Checkout failure under load. Load-test 4–6 weeks out with realistic payment flows.
  </Q>
  <Q q="How much should I discount?">
    UAE shoppers expect 30–50% on hero categories. Below 20% looks stingy; above 60% looks desperate.
  </Q>
  <Q q="Do I need a separate landing page?">
    Yes. `/white-friday` with its own collection, countdown, and tracking. Don't dilute it across the regular site.
  </Q>
  <Q q="What about Cyber Monday in the UAE?">
    Optional extension. Most UAE traffic peaks on White Friday weekend; Cyber Monday is a smaller secondary window.
  </Q>
  <Q q="Should BNPL options be prominent?">
    Yes. Tabby and Tamara drive 25–40% of White Friday GMV in the UAE. Show installment prices on product cards, not just at checkout.
  </Q>
</FAQ>

## Honest summary

White Friday isn't a banner — it's a program. Stores that invest 8 weeks in prep come out ahead on revenue, lower refund rates, and higher customer LTV. Stores that prep for 1 week celebrate the revenue, hate the refunds, and wonder why the next month is flat.
